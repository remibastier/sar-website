Abstract

This paper presents a multi-temporal Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) Doppler Tomography investigation spanning an extensive 8,300 km² synoptic corridor (83 cells), with high-resolution Doppler anomaly detection, clustering, and ground-truth validation focused across 16 retained survey tiles (1,600 km²) encompassing the Memphite-Fayum pyramid necropolises. Initiated to independently evaluate sensational published claims (Biondi, 2022/2025) of massive subterranean caverns and 600-meter deep shafts beneath the Giza Plateau, the investigation demonstrates through multi-temporal repeat-pass interferometry—first across 3 epochs (24 sub-apertures) and expanded to 6 epochs (48 Doppler sub-apertures)—that single-pass cavity detections are predominantly artifacts of radar speckle noise. By temporally stacking six discrete orbital passes across a 30-day temporal baseline, spurious cavity false alarms were reduced by 96.3% across the corridor, demonstrating high phase stability and an absence of anomalous micro-motion signatures within the detection limits of Sentinel-1 C-band interferometry. The research subsequently pivots to classifying genuine surface lithology, exposed megalithic foundations, ancient quarry infrastructure, and shallow paleochannel beds, producing an objective radar survey across 16 active survey tiles, yielding 135 calibrated targets (35 ground-truth benchmark monuments + 100 curated blind discoveries).

2.0 Research Origin & The Biondi Benchmark

FACT In 2022 and 2025, publications by Filippo Biondi claimed the discovery of monumental, deep subterranean chambers and vertical shafts extending up to 600 meters beneath the Giza Plateau, utilizing single-pass satellite SAR Doppler phase analysis.

Our project was established to determine whether an independent, transparent pipeline could reproduce these findings.

DATA RESULT Single-Pass Noise Illusion

When processing a single Sentinel-1 acquisition without multi-temporal stacking, our pipeline initially flagged 4,269 pixels (~0.14 km²) across the Giza Plateau as suspected "voids". However, the mean spatial coherence of this single pass was critically low (γ = 0.098), well below the minimum threshold (γ ≥ 0.15) required for phase reliability.

DATA RESULT When multi-temporal interferometric stacking was applied (first across 4 epochs, then 6 epochs), 100% of the suspected cavity signatures within 500 meters of the Great Pyramids collapsed to zero. Furthermore, direct tomographic probing of the exact Western Cemetery coordinate claimed by Biondi (29.980°N, 31.126°E) revealed absolute bedrock stability with a flat DC power spectrum.

HYPOTHESIS Physical Cause of the Divergence

We hypothesize that earlier published detections were mathematical artifacts caused by radar speckle noise and local surface phase decorrelation. Without multi-temporal temporal cross-correlation across identical repeat orbits, normal microscopic surface roughness produces phase fluctuations that an unconstrained inversion algorithm erroneously interprets as deep resonant cavities.

METHODOLOGICAL NOTE Academic Perspective on the Biondi Benchmark

Our findings do not represent an absolute or hostile refutation of Dr. Filippo Biondi's broader theoretical framework. Differences in acquisition geometry, alternate satellite datasets (e.g. Italian COSMO-SkyMed X-band or ALOS-2 PALSAR L-band), or proprietary mathematical inversion transforms may have contributed to his original observations. Our conclusion is empirical and strictly bounded: under standardized multi-temporal Sentinel-1 C-band interferometry across a 30-day baseline (Relative Orbit 58, 6 epochs, 48 sub-aperture looks), the claimed deep void signatures do not replicate, collapsing by 96.3% into systemic speckle noise and atmospheric phase artifacts.

3.0 Sentinel-1 Constellation & Radar Physics

To maintain scientific integrity, the physical boundaries of satellite C-band radar must be clearly stated:

  • FACT Microwave Penetration Limits: Unlike low-frequency Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR, 10–500 MHz), Sentinel-1 C-band microwaves (5.5 cm) cannot penetrate dense crystalline Mokattam limestone. Direct radar penetration is restricted to dry, low-loss surface sand sheets to a depth of roughly 0.5 to 2.0 meters.
  • FACT Resolution Cell Geometry: Sentinel-1 IW mode has a ground range × azimuth resolution of approximately 5 × 20 meters (~100 m² cell). Features significantly smaller than this cell cannot dominate the backscatter return.
  • DATA RESULT The "Drum-Skin" Micro-Motion Principle: Doppler Tomography does not rely on direct microwave penetration to detect underground cavities. Instead, it measures surface micro-vibrations. A wide, shallow cavern with a flexible roof responds to environmental ambient noise (wind shear, micro-seisms) like a drum skin, creating phase shifts across Doppler sub-apertures.

4.0 Multi-Temporal Analysis: 3-Epoch vs 6-Epoch Extension

4.1 Evolution of the InSAR Processing Baseline (3 Epochs → 6 Epochs)

Our remote sensing pipeline was executed in two consecutive methodological phases. In Phase 1, the Doppler inversion was initially computed across a 3-epoch baseline (August 20, August 26, and September 01, 2026; 24 sub-aperture looks). While this initial 3-epoch stack successfully mapped high-contrast surface monuments (such as the Giza pyramids and Dahshur complexes), it simultaneously generated 1,690 low-coherence "void" candidate pixels across the 10 surveyed regional sectors. In irrigated canal margins (such as the Bahr Yussef adjacent to Hawara) and mobile desert dunes, ephemeral soil moisture fluctuations produced transient phase shifts that closely mimicked subterranean voids.

To rigorously test whether these 1,690 anomalies represented genuine subterranean cavities or transient environmental artifacts, we escalated to Phase 2: expanding the interferometric stack to a full 6-epoch coregistered series (ingesting September 07, 13, and 19, 2026; 48 sub-aperture looks across a 30-day baseline).

Did Extending from 3 to 6 Epochs Break Our Results?

A central question in interferometric remote sensing is whether expanding the temporal baseline introduces temporal decorrelation that breaks established findings, or whether it acts as a decisive physical filter.

DATA RESULT Direct Finding: 6 Epochs Fortified the Science

Extending the stack from 3 to 6 discrete epochs did NOT break our results—it decisively fortified them. In the 3-epoch baseline (24 sub-apertures), transient canal moisture and shifting topsoil had generated 1,690 spurious "void" pixels across the 10 regional scans. When expanded to 6 epochs (48 independent looks across a 30-day baseline, August 20 – September 19, 2026), 1,628 of those 1,690 false pixels vanished completely (-96.3% noise elimination).

DATA RESULT The bedrock plateaus (Giza, Saqqara, Dahshur, Zawyet el-Aryan, and Meidum) maintained high repeat-pass coherence (γ = 0.37 to 0.55), proving that genuine bedrock monuments remain motionless and phase-coherent over monthly baselines.

5.0 Ground-Truth Benchmark Matrix & Radar Detection Physics

Rather than relying on unweighted blind anomaly ranking—where mudbrick superstructures or subtle subterranean chambers risk being masked by intense specular returns from natural limestone escarpments—this investigation implements a rigorous Hypothesis-Driven Ground-Truth Benchmark Matrix. We benchmark 35 culturally, geometrically, and historically documented ancient monuments across the 16 retained survey corridor tiles directly against Sentinel-1 C-band Doppler Tomography data cubes.

📐 Physical Decision Criteria & Mathematical Threshold Rationale

Each benchmark feature is evaluated across four independent physical radar parameters extracted directly from the Sentinel-1 Interferometric Wide (IW) Single Look Complex (SLC) data cubes (nominal multi-looked ground resolution ∼14m × 14m, λ = 5.55 cm, incidence angle θinc ≈ 39.5°):

1. Peak Backscatter (σ₀ ≥ 40.0 dB) Regional hyper-arid limestone baseline has mean μ = 34.20 dB (σ = 4.80 dB). A return ≥40.0 dB represents a >3.8× power increase over desert baseline (+1.2σ), confirming acute dihedral/trihedral retro-reflection from stepped masonry tiers. For weathered mudbrick pyramids (Hawara, Black Pyramid), threshold is calibrated to ≥38.0 dB due to rounded morphology and dielectric clay permittivity, well above surrounding floodplain silt (23–30 dB).
2. Local Prominence (Δσ₀ ≥ +5.0 dB) Target-to-Clutter Ratio (TCR) computed over an immediate 400m radius circular kernel (25×25 pixels): Δσ₀ = σ₀peak − ⟨σ₀⟩r≤400m. A +5.0 dB prominence represents a linear power contrast ratio of 100.5 ≈ 3.162 (>316% contrast over background clutter, >1.0σ), isolating localized structural anomalies from broad regional escarpments.
3. InSAR Coherence (γ ≥ 0.60) 12-day repeat-pass complex interferometric coherence quantifies surface phase stability. Mobile, wind-blown aeolian sands de-correlate rapidly (γ < 0.35), whereas dry-laid monumental limestone blocks and rock-cut foundation terraces maintain sub-millimeter phase integrity (γ ≥ 0.60–0.85). Threshold γ ≥ 0.60 is standard in radar geomorphology to reject sand clutter.
4. Spatial Apex Offset (δr ≤ 85 m) Geodesic distance from documented historical apex/centroid to radar peak. In side-looking radar (θinc ≈ 39.5°), specular corner reflection originates at the base-to-ground dihedral or illuminated facing facet rather than the mathematical summit tip. An offset ≤85m (≤6 radar pixels) corresponds strictly to the pyramid footprint radius (bases 105m to 230m).

METHODOLOGICAL NOTE Academic Perspective on External Remote Sensing & “Metallic Object” Hypotheses

Independent remote sensing researchers (notably Tim Akers / Merlin Burrows in scans presented in 2015 and published by the Mataha Expedition) reported multi-tiered subterranean structural corridors at Hawara, including a central hall hypothesized to house an anomalous 40-meter freestanding “oval metallic object” (“Dippy”).

Our findings under standardized Copernicus Sentinel-1 C-band InSAR Doppler tomography do not replicate a metallic material signature. In microwave physics, a colossal metallic body generates an extreme specular reflection (+75 to +85 dB) with near-total suppression of cross-polarization. Our calibrated radar dataset records peak returns of +48.2 to +50.4 dB with a Dual-Pol Cross-Polarization Ratio of CPR = -3.0 dB, diagnostic of dielectric volume scattering from dense limestone bedrock and fractured stone rubble fill. Crucially, as with our evaluation of the Biondi benchmark, our study does not represent a refutation of their independent findings: Merlin Burrows utilized proprietary optical/multispectral satellite filtering and distinct mathematical processing, whereas our pipeline is strictly bounded by active microwave SAR interferometry (λ = 5.5 cm). Fundamental differences in wavelength, penetration physics, and sensor modalities naturally yield divergent physical sensitivities.

The Osiris Shaft provides the definitive empirical test of whether spaceborne C-band SAR Doppler Tomography can penetrate solid bedrock to detect subterranean voids:

  • Dielectric Attenuation & Skin Depth: Sentinel-1 C-band microwaves (λ = 5.6 cm, f = 5.405 GHz) exhibit an effective penetration depth (δ) of only 0.5 to 1.5 meters in dry, competent Mokattam limestone (εr ≈ 7.0–8.5, loss tangent tan δ ≈ 0.015).   • Groundwater Dielectric Shield: The permanent water table filling Level 3 (εr ≈ 80, high ionic conductivity) acts as a total microwave reflector and absorption sink, preventing any wave propagation beneath the water surface.

12.0 Scientific Hypotheses for Non-Detections & Physical Limitations

While 14 of 21 surveyed royal pyramids and numerous subsurface paleochannels were clearly resolved, certain major monuments (notably the Pyramid of Menkaure at Giza and the Black Pyramid at Dahshur) exhibited radar backscatter and temporal coherence below detection thresholds. We formulate three primary physical hypotheses:

HYPOTHESIS 1 Radar Shadow & Descending Orbit Geometry

Sentinel-1 Relative Orbit 58 is a descending pass (satellite traveling north-to-south, looking west at an incidence angle θ ≈ 39.5°). Steep monumental faces sloping westward (away from the sensor) cast radar shadows, while eastern faces experience geometric layover and foreshortening. For smaller structures located in the lee of larger plateaus, incident radar energy is occluded by adjacent terrain relief.

HYPOTHESIS 2 Why Mudbrick Pyramids Show Weak Radar Contrast

Middle Kingdom mudbrick pyramids (Hawara, Dahshur Black Pyramid) suffer from high microwave attenuation. Sun-dried Nile alluvium possesses higher moisture retention and porous clay structures that absorb microwave energy, resulting in low backscatter that blends into desert wadi washes.

HYPOTHESIS 3 Agricultural Decorrelation Along the Floodplain Edge

C-band radar (5.5 cm) scatters off crop canopies and moist cultivated soils. Temporal changes between 6-day satellite passes scramble the radar phase, creating extreme decorrelation (γ < 0.10). This prevents micro-motion phase analysis along the modern Nile floodplain interface.

13.0 Pending Research Roadmap

To address the physical constraints identified during this C-band campaign, future work will focus on:

  1. Transition to L-Band SAR (JAXA ALOS-2 & NASA-ISRO NISAR): With a longer wavelength (λ ≈ 24 cm), L-band radar penetrates dry desert sand sheets up to 2–5 meters and passes through agricultural crop canopies to reach ground level.
  2. Sub-Meter X-Band Tasking (TerraSAR-X / COSMO-SkyMed): 1-meter spotlight resolution will be deployed over Zawyet el-Aryan and Giza Western Cemetery to detect narrow shafts and mastaba masonry boundaries.
  3. Ascending & Descending Orbit Fusion: Merging east-looking and west-looking acquisitions to eliminate radar shadow on western pyramid slopes.
  4. Ground Truth Fieldwork Collaboration: Cross-verifying the blind discoveries with ground-penetrating radar (GPR) and electrical resistivity tomography (ERT) alongside academic institutions.
